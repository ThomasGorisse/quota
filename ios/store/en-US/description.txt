Quota shows how much of your Claude and Codex usage limits is left, as a ring on your home
screen, your lock screen and your watch. One glance, no app to open.

You run the collector on your own Mac. It reads the usage figures Claude Code and the Codex
CLI already write locally, works out a percentage, and sends that percentage to a small relay.
This app pairs with the relay once, by scanning a QR code, and reads your figures back with
that pairing token. Only percentages and reset times travel: no prompts, no file contents, no
token counts, no account identifiers.

WHAT YOU SEE

• A ring per usage window: the rolling 5-hour one, the weekly one, and the reserve when your
  plan has one.
• Green under 60 % used, amber to 85 %, red above — the same three colours on every screen.
• How long until each window resets, counting down.
• How old the reading is, and a plain STALE marker when the collector has stopped reporting.

WIDGETS

• Home screen, medium: the two windows that matter for each provider, big enough to read at
  arm's length.
• Home screen, small: one ring per provider.
• Lock screen: a circular gauge for whichever window is closest to empty, and a two-line
  rectangular summary.
• Apple Watch: the full set of rings, plus complications in every shape.

LEFT OR USED

One switch turns every ring around: show the capacity left, or the capacity spent. The setting
lives on the backend, so the phone, the widgets, the watch and the web dashboard all follow at
once. The colours never move — a window that is red stays red in both readings.

PRIVACY

No account. No analytics. No crash reporter. No third-party SDK. No tracking. The app talks to
exactly one host: the one in the pairing URL you gave it. The camera is used only to read that
pairing QR code, and no image is stored.

WHAT YOU NEED

A Mac where you use Claude Code or the Codex CLI, and the free open-source collector from
github.com/ThomasGorisse/quota. Without it this app has nothing to show.
